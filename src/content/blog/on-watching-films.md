The first time I watch a film, I'm mostly following the plot. Where is this going,
who is lying to whom, will the thing in the box explode. It's a nervous kind of
attention — half anticipation, half the low-grade anxiety of not knowing.

The second viewing is completely different.

## Plot is the least interesting layer

Once the story is settled, the film has to survive on everything else. Editing
rhythm. How long a shot holds before the cut. Whether the characters are ever
allowed to just sit in a room together without the plot knocking on the door.

Some films collapse on a rewatch. You notice the plot was the only thing holding it
up, and without the suspense it's just a sequence of scenes that happened. Others
get *denser*. You start watching the background. You notice a gesture in an early
scene that pays off ninety minutes later, and you realise the director was playing
a much longer game than you thought.

## The films that reward it

A few that got better the second time, and why:

- **In the Mood for Love** — first time I was waiting for them to act. Second time
  I understood the film is about the not-acting, and the hallway shots are the point.
- **Parasite** — the staircase motif is obvious in retrospect, invisible on first
  watch because the tone shifts are so effective.
- **Perfect Days** — almost nothing happens, which makes the first viewing feel
  slow and the second feel like a meditation.

## An argument for fewer films, watched more

I used to chase the next thing. Now I'd rather sit with five films a year and
actually learn their grammar. There's a difference between having seen something
and knowing it, and the gap between those two only closes on the second viewing.
