Building in public has a well-known set of benefits: feedback, accountability, and
the small compounding effect of being findable. I'm a fan. But there's a second
activity that gets less airtime, and I think it matters more.

## The private notebook

Before anything is fit to publish, there's a mess. Wrong assumptions, half-built
ideas, code that doesn't work, a strong opinion I held for two days. That mess
needs somewhere to live that isn't a public commit history.

If you only ever write in public, everything you write is a performance. You start
choosing topics based on what performs, and the thinking gets shaped by the audience
before it's even had a chance to be wrong in private.

## A rough split

- **Private notebook** — daily, ugly, no structure. Questions I'm stuck on, quotes
  worth keeping, snippets that may never ship. Never intended for anyone.
- **Public writing** — occasional, edited, only when the thinking has settled enough
  to be useful to a stranger.

The notebook feeds the writing. Most of it is discarded, which is the correct
outcome. Discarding is cheap in a notebook and expensive in public.

## What actually gets published

Usually something small that took a surprising amount of work, explained plainly
enough that someone else can skip the work. That's the whole transaction. It's not
a diary and it's not a portfolio piece — it's a note left on a trail for whoever
comes after.

Keeping that distinction clear is, for me, the difference between writing that's
worth reading and writing that's just visible.
